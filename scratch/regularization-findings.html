Decision: keep the existing recipe as a control. The implementation already separates sampled-ratio clipping, collection-policy KL and fixed-reference KL. The retained MTG-105 screen does not compare these mechanisms. Before attributing entropy changes to regularization, measure distributions at identical decisions, including action-encoding collisions. What the optimizer actually compares

Entry points and data flow

uv run manabot train --regime ... --out ... validates a TrainingRegime and dispatches through execute_regime. The MTG-92/93/105 controls use its ordinary TrainSelfPlay stage, SeatRoutedCollector, and the optimizer owned by NetOpponentTrainer/Trainer. Learning selects the PPO regime update; AtaraxosMoveLearning selects the damped move update. The ordinary uv run manabot train --preset ... path instead reaches the stock trainer. Calling all three paths simply “PPO” obscures meaningful differences.

Each collector stream saves the acting learner’s observation, sampled action, selected log probability, full masked behavior probabilities and normalized log probabilities, and scalar or categorical outcome prediction. A transition ends at that same learner’s next decision or game termination; intervening opponent actions get no learner policy-gradient row. Terminal rewards are from the learner’s perspective, and truncation fails rather than becoming a draw. The next observation is paused before sampling its action.

Parameters are fixed during collection. Raw-self uses current learner weights for self-play; EMA-self uses averaged weights and bootstraps from that same pre-update averaged model. Games can span updates. The saved behavior is the policy that actually sampled the action, not a reconstructed “previous model.” These are fresh, close-to-on-policy batches, not full episodes under immutable weights and not trajectory-level importance correction.

Let p be the current legal-action distribution, b the saved collection distribution, rho the prior at the same observation, and a the sampled action. For a retained actor row the move loss is

Lπ=−min⁡(rÂ,clip⁡(r,1−ϵ,1+ϵ)Â)+β∑j∈Lpjlog⁡(pj/bj)+τ∑j∈Lpjlog⁡(pj/ρj),r=pa/ba. L_\pi=-\min(r\hat A,\operatorname{clip}(r,1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\hat A) +\beta\sum_{j\in L}p_j\log(p_j/b_j) +\tau\sum_{j\in L}p_j\log(p_j/\rho_j),\qquad r=p_a/b_a.

Both sums are KL(current || target); “reverse KL” in repository names means this explicit orientation. Clipping sees one sampled ratio. The KL terms see every legal offer at that row. Neither is a hard bound on the final update. There is no exact optimization over a free policy simplex: Adam updates shared network parameters, with gradient clipping, minibatches and a critic loss. This is not a tabular mirror-descent step.

For the move recipe, detached policy advantages use gamma=1 and lambda=.5; value targets use lambda=.8. The categorical critic learns soft loss/draw/win lambda targets by cross-entropy; its scalar ablation uses mean squared error. The policy and critic losses have equal weight. Inclusive absolute-advantage quantile .75 plus floor .01 select rows before training. One epoch processes increasing learner-timestep minibatches, without advantage normalization. Actual ratios matter even in one epoch because earlier timestep minibatches change the learner before later ones.

Behavior, references, advantages and targets are constants for differentiation. Current policy/value logits and shared encoder receive gradients. The default filter applies to actor and critic. With filter_scope="actor", the surrogate and both KLs use selected actor rows but the critic uses all rows. An empty actor set therefore does not prevent policy movement through the shared encoder. An empty actor+critic set skips Adam; the EMA iteration clock still advances.

Adam uses inherited betas .9/.999 and epsilon 1e-5. The move defaults are ratio radius .2, collection KL coefficient .1, max gradient norm .267, LR(i)=clip(.5/i^1.1, 5e-6, 1e-4) and tau(i)=.05/i^.3, on a one-based collection/update clock. EMA .999 advances after the update, including skips. Gradient norm clipping acts on the joint loss before Adam; it is not a bound on parameter displacement or policy KL. Numerical health measures parameter movement on the first optimizer step at iteration 1 and every 25th iteration.

The PPO regime uses the same two KL types but independently configurable traces, selected-row advantage normalization, shuffled multi-epoch minibatches, and .5*MSE times value_weight. Its configured progress clock can be elapsed budget or iteration fraction. The stock preset trainer instead has an explicit -ent_coef*H(p) bonus, optional clipped value loss, and sampled mean((r-1)-log(r)) for target-KL early stopping. Under exact expectation over b, that estimator corresponds to KL(b || p), not the regime’s full KL(p || b). Finite-sample stopping and ratio clipping still do not enforce a hard trust region. See stock _optimize_step.

Why reference choice changes the scientific question

Encoded-uniform means rho(a)=1/N for N legal offers. At a fixed observation,

KL(p∥UniformN)=log⁡N−H(p). KL(p\|\mathrm{Uniform}_N)=\log N-H(p).

Thus fixed-reference KL to uniform and an entropy bonus of the same coefficient have identical policy gradients on the same rows, although their logged losses differ. This is a myopic state loss, not entropy-to-go. Changing the collector, selected rows, normalization, optimizer, or schedules invalidates an “entropy versus KL” equivalence claim for the whole experiment.

The implemented structured prior is only action-type uniform. If G types are present and type g has n_g offers, rho(a)=1/(G*n_g). It gives equal total mass to each type, then equal mass within that type. Its KL decomposes into family-mass KL to uniform plus policy-weighted conditional within-family KL. Equivalently it is -H(p)+log G+E_p[log n_g]. This compensates offer counts at the type level; it does not balance source cards, targets, modal semantics or complete payment outcomes within a type. A “fixed” prior is a fixed rule conditioned on the state’s legal offers, not one action vector for every state.

The prior can resist loss of a rare type, but can also overweight pass, decline or poor types. It cannot certify useful exploration or equilibrium mixing. Collection KL instead resists departure from whatever behavior already does, including bad or collapsed behavior. At p=b its gradient is zero; after earlier minibatches, or initially under EMA behavior, it can be nonzero. The uniform prior can still exert a gradient at that same point.

Legal support, encodings and entropy

The engine owns legality. The ordinary encoder emits a one-hot action type, validity, and object-focus indices. Its capacity validation rejects too many offers/focus objects before the internal bounded encoding loop; the .take inside that loop alone is not evidence of silent legal-action truncation. The MTG-105 receipt uses a 64-action capacity. Runtime schemas must be pinned; neither a smaller tensor nor a subset of offers is an interchangeable world.

Important counterexample to the guide’s general encoding concern: compute_player_actions emits a cast per castable card, followed later by target/payment prompts. It does not enumerate every target/payment combination as a priority action. More targets therefore do not directly increase that spell’s priority mass. They can increase conditional choice entropy and the number of subsequent training rows. Ordinary mana-source payment is not represented as a Cartesian menu here.

Waterbend can have multiple sequential tap paths to the same selected set. Whether two paths are strategically equivalent requires checking final state, triggers and information, not comparing labels. The structured-offer path already canonicalizes selected tap IDs and rejects duplicates (InternalOffer::Waterbend); do not assume those permutation aliases survive there. Different tapped permanents can matter strategically. Repeated copies are not automatically redundant.

Observed representation, derived policy consequence: flow/tick.rs offers true/false for the same attacker. Observation::action_focus gives both the same permanent; ActionOption.declared retains the distinction for presentation, but encode_actions does not encode that field. The ordinary Agent scores identical type/focus features with shared row-wise layers. Consequently their logits coincide for all weights, giving exactly 50/50 stochastic play when those are the two offers. This is not learned equilibrium mixing. Modal choices similarly omit mode index, and multiple activated abilities on one permanent omit ability index from the ordinary action features. Their occurrence in the screened matchup is not measured here. These are feature collisions between potentially different outcomes, distinct from redundant encodings of the same outcome.

The separate compound trainer is not a silent fix or drop-in control: optimize_games regularizes conditional distributions at retained behavior prefixes. Summing those conditionals does not give exact joint KL(new || behavior), which needs new-policy prefix occupancy. Conditional uniform is not uniform over completed subsets. Keep compound factorization and search-specific trust regularization outside this comparison.

Support: both implemented priors are positive on every legal offer and zero on padding. policy_logs uses a legal mask, finite normalized legal logs and -inf on padding. Invalid masks, empty support and inconsistent stored behavior are rejected. Move loss rejects zero prior probability on legal actions. In the mathematical forward-from-current KL, a true target zero with positive current mass makes KL infinite; a zero-support scripted prior would prohibit those actions. It would need an explicit full-support mixture, not an epsilon introduced unnoticed into this experiment.

Finite float32 sampling can nevertheless underflow legal exp(log p) to zero. The saved legal log remains finite, KL uses logs, and health counts underflow; there is no probability floor. Thus nominal log-space support and realized exploration differ. A sampled action must have positive saved sampling mass. These invariants are covered by existing numerical-health and gradient tests, not newly demonstrated at a trained checkpoint here.

Source-check of the paper claims that affect this decision

The final Nature Methods explicitly separates prior regularization from four update controls: collection-policy KL, ratio clipping, gradient clipping and Adam learning rate. The move prior samples a movable piece then a legal move; the setup process uses entropy. Ablations remove components from the final configuration without retuning other hyperparameters. They support usefulness within that configuration, not superiority of coordinated schedules over independently retuned schedules or transfer to MTG. Sokota et al., Nature, Methods and Extended Data Fig. 5.

The supplement S3.4, equation (6), directly contains the clipped surrogate and both oriented KL terms. Equation (5) is categorical outcome cross-entropy. Table S7 specifies .2 ratio clipping, .1 collection KL, .267 gradient cap, .5/.8 traces, the two iteration power laws, one epoch and .999 evaluation EMA. This confirms the implemented recipe’s stated source, with MTG changes to action grouping, decision clock and model. It does not supply an equilibrium guarantee for this neural implementation. Supplement, S3.4 and Table S7.

The distinction from mirror descent is substantive: the MMD paper’s equilibrium and tabular results concern its specified algorithm, not arbitrary Adam losses containing KL. PPO likewise introduces a surrogate optimization method, not a universal final-policy KL constraint. MMD paper, PPO paper. Search-specific objectives and ablation interpretation remain with the MMD task; no training recommendation here depends on the guide’s search-result claim.
